# Stage 02 — Derivatives of Theta Functions

Stage 02 is the arithmetic engine that makes prime numbers visible inside geometry. Where [Stage 01](../01_reduction/overview.md) showed that a one-sided ceiling on the weighted prime-counting remainder $R_{q_0}(y)$ implies both GRH and RH, Stage 02 explains *where the prime-counting weight $\Lambda(n)(1 - \chi_K(n))$ actually comes from*: derivatives of elliptic theta functions on a CM elliptic curve over $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ automatically accumulate $p$-adic divisibility at every non-split prime $p$ with the exact slope $\gamma_p$ matching that weight, and Stage 02 proves both the lower bound on this divisibility, the existence of a globally integral theta vacuum, and the sharpness of the divisibility law.

## Level 1 — First-Principles

Why would taking repeated derivatives of a smooth periodic wave know anything about {{concept:prime-number|whole numbers greater than $1$ with no smaller positive divisors}}? Ordinarily, taking $n$ derivatives of a power series $\sum c_k z^k$ at $z = 0$ multiplies $c_n$ by $n!$. Because $n!$ is divisible by roughly $p^{n/(p-1)}$ for every prime $p$, ordinary calculus gives every prime the same baseline rate of divisibility, $1/(p-1)$ per derivative, with no way to distinguish one arithmetic family of primes from another.

Stage 02 breaks that symmetry by working on an elliptic curve with {{concept:complex-multiplication|extra algebraic symmetries coming from the integer ring of an imaginary quadratic field}} $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ (with prime $d \equiv 7 \pmod 8$). The basic building block on this {{term:complex-multiplication|CM curve}} $\mathcal{E} = \mathbb{C}/\Omega_0\mathcal{O}_K$ is its {{concept:elliptic-theta-function|quasi-periodic entire function on the lattice quotient whose Taylor coefficients encode the geometry of the curve}}. When you measure the divisibility of its derivatives by powers of a prime $p$ — its {{concept:arithmetic-height|prime-by-prime divisibility exponent tracked alongside ordinary size}} (its {{term:arithmetic-height|$p$-adic valuation}}) — the local geometry of the curve near $0$ behaves like a {{concept:formal-group-law|power-series addition rule near the origin whose $p$-th-power behavior reflects how $p$ factors in $K$}}:

- **Split primes ($\chi_K(p) = 1$):** The curve behaves ordinarily modulo $p$. The scaling radius of the local coordinate cancels the full factorial rate $1/(p-1)$, leaving **extra slope $\gamma_p = 0$**.
- **Inert primes ($\chi_K(p) = -1$):** The curve becomes supersingular modulo $p$, so multiplication by $p$ behaves like a $p^2$-th power rather than a $p$-th power. Rescaling the variable only costs $\rho_p = \frac{1}{p^2 - 1}$ per derivative, leaving a sharp **extra slope**
  $$\gamma_p = \frac{1}{p-1} - \frac{1}{p^2-1} = \frac{p}{p^2 - 1} = \frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{p^3} + \frac{1}{p^5} + \cdots$$
- **The ramified prime ($p = d$, $\chi_K(d) = 0$):** Multiplication by $\pi_d = \sqrt{-d}$ has half a prime's divisibility ($v(\pi_d) = 1/2$) and degree $d$, costing $\rho_d = \frac{1}{2(d-1)}$ and leaving **extra slope**
  $$\gamma_d = \frac{1}{d-1} - \frac{1}{2(d-1)} = \frac{1}{2(d-1)} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{d^2} + \frac{1}{d^3} + \cdots\right).$$

Multiply each slope by $2 \log p$ and the hidden prime sum snaps into focus: for every prime $p$, the extra divisibility per derivative is *identically*
$$2 \gamma_p \log p = \sum_{e \ge 1} \frac{\Lambda(p^e)\bigl(1 - \chi_K(p^e)\bigr)}{p^e},$$
combining {{concept:von-mangoldt-function|the logarithmic step weight $\Lambda(n)$ equal to $\log p$ on powers of a prime $p$ and $0$ elsewhere}} with {{concept:quadratic-twist|the sign-flipping quadratic-residue weight $\chi_K(n)$ separating split, inert, and ramified primes}}. That single identity is the central arithmetic mechanism of the proof: the exact prime-counting weight that Stage 01 feeds into {{concept:landaus-lemma|the positivity test forbidding zeros to the right of $\operatorname{Re}(s) = 1/2$}} is literally the $p$-adic slope of derivatives on a {{term:complex-multiplication|CM curve}}.

To turn this one-variable mechanism into an engine for the multi-variable automorphic theta lifts of Stages 03–07 over the cyclotomic tower $E = K(\zeta_{q_1})$, Stage 02 assembles five landmarks into three tightly interlocked pieces:

1. **Normal form and prime-counting divisibility (`theta_products`, `count`):** Over the cyclotomic CM field $E$, taking a product of copies of the one-variable {{term:elliptic-theta-function}} along a {{concept:tight-frame|redundant spanning family of vectors reconstructing every vector from weighted projections}} collapses its exponential factor into a {{concept:theta-normal-form|canonical factorization pairing each infinite place with its shifted Galois partner through homogeneous couplings}}. A clever linear change of directions then forces those {{term:theta-normal-form|Frobenius couplings}} to vanish modulo $\pi_p$, making the rescaled series integral so Legendre's factorial bound yields explicit divisibility lower bounds on every {{concept:schur-jet|derivative of a theta Taylor series along matrix minors and Schur polynomials}}.
2. **{{term:tight-frame|Tight frames}} and the globally integral vacuum (`tight_frames`, `integral_vacuum`):** The multi-variable automorphic theta function of a Hermitian space starts from a Gaussian "vacuum" vector whose arithmetic values have unknown denominators. Using the powers of $\zeta_{q_1}$ as an explicit {{term:tight-frame}}, Stage 02 expresses a multiple of the vacuum as a finite sum of translates of an integral {{term:elliptic-theta-function|theta product}}, producing a single rescaled vector $\theta^\circ$ — the **integral vacuum** — whose finite-point values are integral at *every* prime while all their conjugates remain bounded by $\exp(C q_1^2 n)$.
3. **Sharpness of the divisibility bound (`bound_attained`):** Knowing that derivatives are *at least* as divisible as the predicted prime sum feeds Theorem A (Stage 03), while Theorem C (Stages 05–06) needs the reverse direction — that no hidden extra divisibility inflates the arithmetic floor. Using a $12$-copy construction built from a {{term:tight-frame}} of length $M$ and Fourier inversion back to indicator data, Stage 02 proves that the predicted prime-sum divisibility is actually *attained* up to a tiny controlled factor.

### Where this analogy breaks

Thinking of Stage 02 as a one-variable derivative slope $\gamma_p$ multiplied across independent coordinates hides the main multi-variable obstruction. On the cyclotomic field $E = K(\zeta_{q_1})$, the {{term:theta-normal-form|Frobenius couplings}} $\Gamma_e$ do *not* stay diagonal at a single infinite place $\tau$; they couple $\tau$ to its shifted {{term:theta-normal-form|partner place}} $\tau' = -p^e \tau$. As a result, a derivative of order $a_\tau$ at $\tau$ cannot earn the full baseline $\gamma_p a_\tau$: whenever the {{term:theta-normal-form|partner place}} $\tau'$ also carries derivatives, killing the cross-coupling modulo $\pi_p$ costs a determinant deduction, reducing the net {{term:schur-jet|predicted divisibility}} to positive differences like $(a_\tau/p^e - a_{\tau'} - a'_{\tau'})_+$. It is precisely this partner-place cancellation that later encodes the cutoff shift $n \mapsto -n\tau$ in Stage 01's prime sums.

1. **From formal groups to the theta normal form (`FormalGroup/FrobeniusCongruence`, `Exponent`, `Resolution`, `ThetaProducts`, `Divisibility/NormalForm`):**
   At any non-split prime $p$ ($\chi_K(p) \neq 1$; since $d \equiv 7 \pmod 8$, $p$ is odd, and for admissible data with $18480 \mid q_0$, every non-split prime satisfies $p \ge 13$), the formal group of $\mathcal{E}$ has Frobenius norm $q_p = p^2$ (inert) or $q_p = d$ (ramified), uniformizer $\pi_p = p$ or $\sqrt{-d}$ of valuation $v(\pi_p) \in \{1, 1/2\}$, and scaling valuation
   $$\rho_p = \frac{v(\pi_p)}{q_p - 1} = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{p^2 - 1} & \text{if } \chi_K(p) = -1, \\ \frac{1}{2(d - 1)} & \text{if } p = d, \end{cases} \qquad \gamma_p = \frac{1}{p - 1} - \rho_p = \begin{cases} \frac{p}{p^2 - 1} & \text{if } \chi_K(p) = -1, \\ \frac{1}{2(d - 1)} & \text{if } p = d. \end{cases}$$
   The Frobenius congruence $\theta(\pi_p z)/\theta(z)^{q_p} \equiv 1 \pmod{\pi_p}$ in $\mathcal{O}[[y]]$ forces $\theta(\varpi z) = W \exp(c_\varpi \Xi_1)$ with $v(\varpi) = \rho_p$, $c_\varpi = \varpi^{p+1}/p$, and $v(c_\varpi \pi_p) = \frac{1}{p-1}$. Over the cyclotomic CM field $E = K(\zeta_{q_1})$ with infinite places $\Phi_s \cong \operatorname{Gal}(E/K) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/q_1\mathbb{Z})^\times$, the partner map $\tau' = -p^e \tau$ (`P.partner s p e τ`) satisfies $\tau(\alpha)^{p^e} \equiv \overline{\tau'(\alpha)} \pmod{\pi_p}$ for $e \in I_p^\circ$ (`norm_pow_sub_conj_partner_le`). For any $p$-integral resolution of the identity $z = \sum_j m_j (u_j^\dagger z) u_j$, summing the exponents yields `New.Parameters.exists_hasNormalForm`:
   $$\Bigl(\prod_j \theta(u_j^\dagger z)^{m_j} \cdot H\Bigr)(\varpi z) = W_{\mathrm{tot}}(z)\,\exp\!\Bigl(c_\varpi \sum_{e \in I_p^\circ} \sum_{j \ge 0} \lambda_{e,j} \Gamma_e^{p^j}\Bigr), \qquad \Gamma_e = \sum_{(\tau, c)} z_{(\tau,c)}^{p^e} z_{(-p^e\tau, c)}.$$

2. **From normal forms to Schur-jet divisibility (`Divisibility/NullFrames`, `Count`, `Vacuum/Divisibility`):**
   Given Young diagrams $\kappa_\tau$ of columns of height $h < N = |n'|$, a standard filling $i'$, and integral matrices $y_\tau$, `New.minorProd i' y` builds the product of minors $P_y(z)$ of total degree $B = \mathrm{boxes}(\kappa, N)$. Legendre's factorial bound (`norm_jet_le_of_isHomogeneous`) minus the chain-rule determinant cost yields `norm_jet_minorProd_le_of_charges` and its unmarked form `norm_jet_minorProd_le_of_hasNormalForm`:
   $$v_p\bigl(P_y(\partial)F(0)\bigr) \ge \mathrm{predictedDivisibility}_p(\kappa) - f N^3 \bigl(1 + \log_p \max(B, 1)\bigr).$$
   Finally, `exists_norm_schurJet_le_norm_jet_minorProd` bounds every entry of the Schur-matrix jet `schurJet κ F i' i` by some integral minor product $P_y$, and `Vacuum/Divisibility.lean::norm_schurJet_jetSeries_le_of_charges` applies this directly to standard data of level one for Stage 03.

   - `IntegralVacuum.lean` uses Fourier inversion over the lattice $q_1 \mathfrak{a}_0^n$ (`exists_smul_vacElem_eq_sum`) to write a nonzero multiple of the vacuum as a sum of $(q_1^2)^{fn}$ Heisenberg translates of a level-one theta product, yielding `vacuumScale` and the integral vacuum $\theta^\circ = \mathrm{vacuumScale}^n \cdot \mathrm{vacElem}$ (`intVacuum`) satisfying `norm_intVacuum_le_one`, `norm_ringEquiv_intVacuum_le` ($\|\sigma(\theta^\circ(\mathrm{finVec}\ x))\| \le \exp(C q_1^2)^n$), and Galois jet conjugation (`exists_isJetConjugate_translates`).
