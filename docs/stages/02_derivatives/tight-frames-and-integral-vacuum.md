# Tight Frames, Chowla–Selberg Scaling & the Integral Vacuum $\theta^\circ$

This page explains how Stage 02 transfers one-dimensional arithmetic control from a single CM elliptic curve into high-dimensional adelic theta spaces (`src/New/Derivatives/Vacuum/TightFrames.lean` and `src/New/Derivatives/Vacuum/IntegralVacuum.lean`), constructing the globally integral, conjugate-bounded vacuum $\theta^\circ = \mathrm{intVacuum}$ and proving the Galois conjugation law for translated jet series. Return to the [Stage 02 Overview](./overview.md), see how $\theta^\circ$ feeds the divisibility count in [Formal Groups, Theta Normal Form & Prime-Counting Divisibility](./formal-group-and-divisibility.md), or continue to [Sharpness: Proving the Valuation Bound Is Attained](./sharpness-bound-attained.md).

## Level 1 — First-Principles

Inside the adelic theta spaces of Stage 02, the raw Gaussian vacuum vector lives in a high-dimensional space built from all infinite places of a cyclotomic field $E$. On its own, that vacuum is a purely analytic object: you cannot tell directly whether its values are integral at every {{concept:prime-number|indivisible whole number greater than $1$}}, nor can you bound how large its algebraic conjugates become under symmetries of the {{concept:complex-plane|two-dimensional number system of real and imaginary parts}}. To control both integrality and size at once, the proof builds a bridge from a single one-dimensional curve up to the full high-dimensional space in four steps:

1. **Fix one arithmetic anchor curve.** Choose a single one-dimensional torus $\mathcal{E} = \mathbb{C} / \Omega_0 \mathcal{O}_K$ carrying {{concept:complex-multiplication|extra geometric symmetries coming from an imaginary quadratic ring of integers}}—a {{term:complex-multiplication|CM curve}} whose period grid is $\Omega_0 \mathcal{O}_K$. The classical Chowla–Selberg identity pins down the area of every Galois conjugate of this {{term:complex-multiplication|CM lattice}} up to a single global scaling factor $u$ whose size is $1$ at every prime, linking the curve's period $\Omega_0$ directly to Euler's constant and the logarithmic derivative of the quadratic $L$-value at $1$.
2. **Decompose the high-dimensional geometry using roots of unity.** Just as the equally spaced vertices of a regular polygon let you recover any vector's squared length as an equal-weighted sum of one-dimensional projections, the powers $1, \zeta, \zeta^2, \dots, \zeta^{q_1 - 1}$ of a primitive $q_1$-th root of unity form a {{concept:tight-frame|redundant collection of directions whose one-dimensional projections sum back to the exact identity metric}} (a {{term:tight-frame|resolution of the identity}}), simply by orthogonality of roots of unity. Pulling back one-dimensional {{concept:elliptic-theta-function|quasi-periodic entire functions attached to a one-dimensional torus}} along these directions multiplies one-dimensional sections into a high-dimensional {{term:elliptic-theta-function|theta product}}. Because the pulled-back one-dimensional metrics sum to the exact identity metric, the resulting multivariate Taylor expansion—its {{concept:schur-jet|multivariate Taylor series recording all partial derivatives at the origin}}—factors cleanly into a product of one-dimensional Taylor series evaluated along linear forms.
3. **Average over finite Heisenberg shifts to capture $\theta^\circ$.** A {{term:elliptic-theta-function|theta product}} built from the anchor {{term:complex-multiplication|CM curve}} carries an extra level factor $q_1$, so it is not yet the pure vacuum. Averaging it over a finite family of $(q_1^2)^{fn}$ Heisenberg shifts projects it squarely onto a nonzero scalar multiple $c \cdot \mathrm{vacElem}$ of the vacuum. Because every shifted copy is integral at every prime and every torsion value of the one-dimensional {{term:elliptic-theta-function}} has uniformly bounded conjugates over $K$, the averaged sum inherits both global integrality and an explicit exponential bound on all its conjugates. Multiplying $n$ copies of this rank-one normalization produces the globally integral, conjugate-bounded vacuum $\theta^\circ$.
4. **Track Galois symmetries across all translated jets.** Because the translates of that level-one {{term:elliptic-theta-function|theta product}} span the entire finite part of the space, every symmetry $\sigma$ over $K$ carries the {{term:schur-jet|jet series}} of any translate at the fixed period scale `jetScale` to the {{term:schur-jet|jet series}} of a companion translate on the conjugate {{term:complex-multiplication|CM curve}}, while Chowla–Selberg forces the squared size of the conjugated scale to equal a single universal spectral constant $\mathrm{bigOmega}^{-1}$ independent of $\sigma$.

### Where this analogy breaks

Comparing the powers of $\zeta$ to the vertices of a regular polygon makes the {{term:tight-frame}} sound purely Euclidean. In fact, the frame vectors must also satisfy an arithmetic integrality condition inside the ideal lattice $\mathfrak{a}_0$ (integral under the Weil idele $\alpha^{-1}$) so that every pullback maps period vectors into the period lattice of the {{term:complex-multiplication|CM curve}} and forces every finite value of the {{term:elliptic-theta-function|theta product}} to be integral at every prime.

Likewise, calling the Heisenberg sum an "average" might suggest dividing by the group size $(q_1^2)^{fn}$, which would introduce huge denominators at primes dividing $q_1$. The proof never divides by $(q_1^2)^{fn}$: it keeps the *unnormalized* finite sum and absorbs the resulting nonzero constant $c$ directly into `vacuumScale`. That preserves $p$-adic integrality at every prime without exception, at the modest archimedean cost of a conjugate bound $\exp(C q_1^2 n)$ that is tiny compared to the main growth parameters of Stage 02.

Finally, Chowla–Selberg scaling does not make the transcendental period $\Omega_0$ algebraic. Instead, `jetScale` pairs the period $\Omega_0$ with the Chowla–Selberg unit $u$ and the area factor $\sqrt{2\pi/\sqrt{d}}$ so that under any automorphism $\sigma$ over $K$, the non-algebraic period of the conjugate {{term:complex-multiplication|CM curve}} cancels cleanly against $\sigma(u)$, pinning the squared norm of the conjugated scale to the purely spectral constant $\mathrm{bigOmega}^{-1}$.

## Level 2 — Undergrad Bridge

### 1. The anchor CM curve, Chowla–Selberg unit, and jet scaling

In `src/New/Derivatives/Vacuum/TightFrames.lean` (`lines 85–130`), the proof fixes once and for all a single CM elliptic curve attached to the imaginary quadratic field $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ ($d = P.d \equiv 7 \pmod 8$ prime):
- **`New.Parameters.cmCurve`** ($\mathcal{E} = \mathbb{C}/\Omega_0\mathcal{O}_K$) and **`New.Parameters.cmPeriod`** ($\Omega_0 \in \mathbb{C}^\times$), linked by **`New.Parameters.latticeModule_cmCurve`**: the lattice module of `P.cmCurve` is $\Omega_0 \cdot 1 = \Omega_0 \mathcal{O}_K$.
- **`New.Parameters.chowlaSelbergUnit`** ($u \in \mathbb{C}$), chosen from `CMLattice.chowla_selberg` so that:
  1. **`norm_iota_chowlaSelbergUnit`**: $\|\iota_p(u)\| = 1$ at every prime $p$ and every $p$-adic embedding $\iota_p : \mathbb{C} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}_p$, and
  2. **`area_mul_norm_chowlaSelbergUnit_sq`**: for every $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/K)$,
     $$\operatorname{area}(\mathcal{E}^\sigma) \cdot \|\sigma u\|^2 = \pi \exp\bigl(\gamma - \operatorname{Re}\tfrac{L'}{L}(1, \chi_K)\bigr) \cdot \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{d}}.$$
- Three derived scaling constants for the cyclotomic tower level $E = P.E\ s = K(\zeta_{q_1})$ (with $f$ infinite places and Weil idele $\alpha = P.\alpha\ s$):
  $$\mathrm{areaScale} := \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{d}}} \in \mathbb{C}^\times, \qquad \mathrm{periodFactor} := \frac{\mathrm{vacuumRoot}}{u}, \qquad \mathrm{jetScale} := \frac{\mathrm{periodFactor} \cdot \mathrm{areaScale}}{\Omega_0}.$$
  Here `vacuumRoot` is $(\chi_{\mathrm{fin}}(\alpha))^{-1/f}$ (principal branch), where $\chi_{\mathrm{fin}}(x) = \upsilon(x)|x|^{-1/2}$ is the finite-idele character attached to the Weil splitting character $\upsilon$. Because $u$ is a $p$-adic unit everywhere, `periodFactor` has the exact same $p$-adic norm as `vacuumRoot`:
  $$\|\iota_p(\mathrm{periodFactor})\| = \|\mathrm{adeleExtension}(\mathrm{weightEmbedding}\ \iota_p\ \tau)(\alpha)\|^{-1}$$
  (`norm_iota_periodFactor`), which equals $1$ whenever $p \nmid q_1$ (`norm_iota_periodFactor_eq_one`) and guarantees $\|\iota_p(\mathrm{periodFactor} \cdot \overline{\Phi_\tau(y)})\| \le 1$ for every $y \in \mathfrak{a}_0 = E \cap \alpha \widehat{\mathcal{O}}_E$ (`norm_periodFactor_mul_conj_le_one`).

On the archimedean side, `New.Parameters.norm_gal_periodFactor_div_mul_areaScale_sq` (`IntegralVacuum.lean:469–489`) combines `area_mul_norm_chowlaSelbergUnit_sq` with the definition of `bigOmega`: whenever the conjugate lattice $\mathcal{E}^\sigma$ has period parallelogram area $|\Omega|^2 \sqrt{d}/2$,
$$\left\|\frac{\sigma(\mathrm{periodFactor})}{\Omega} \cdot \mathrm{areaScale}\right\|^2 = (\mathrm{bigOmega}\ P\ s)^{-1}, \qquad \mathrm{bigOmega}\ P\ s = e^{\gamma - \operatorname{Re} L'/L(1, \chi_K)} \frac{2\pi}{\operatorname{rootDiscr}(E)} > 0.$$
Thus the transcendental period $\Omega$ cancels completely, leaving a positive spectral constant independent of $\sigma$.

### 2. Roots-of-unity tight frames, resolution products, and jet factorization

A family $w : I \to E$ with multiplicities $m : I \to \mathbb{N}$ forms a **tight frame** of length $M$ (`IsTightFrame w m M`) if every $w_j$ has integral finite image $\alpha^{-1} w_j \in \widehat{\mathcal{O}}_E$ and
$$\sum_{j \in I} m_j \, \Phi_\sigma(w_j) \overline{\Phi_\tau(w_j)} = M \, \delta_{\sigma\tau} \qquad (\forall \sigma, \tau \in \Phi).$$
Because distinct CM embeddings disagree on a primitive $q_1$-th root of unity (`eq_of_phi_eq_of_isPrimitiveRoot`), orthogonality of roots of unity gives **`New.Parameters.isTightFrame_pow`**: for any primitive $q_1$-th root $z \in E$, the powers $(z^i)_{0 \le i < q_1}$ form a tight frame of length $q_1$ with all multiplicities $1$. Scaling multiplicities by $k$ (`New.IsTightFrame.mul_left`) yields **`New.Parameters.isTightFrame_zeta_pow`**, a tight frame $(\zeta^i)_{0 \le i < q_1}$ of length $k q_1$.

Given a tight frame $w$ of length $M$, attach to each pair $i = (j, k) \in I \times n$ the linear form `resolutionLinearForm (b / P.areaScale) w i` sending $z \mapsto \frac{b}{\mathrm{areaScale}} \sum_\tau \overline{\Phi_\tau(w_j)} z_{\tau, k}$ and the shift `resolutionShift b w γ i` $= -b \sum_\tau \Phi_\tau(\gamma_k)\overline{\Phi_\tau(w_j)}$. Three lemmas connect one-dimensional sections on a CM lattice $C$ to the $fn$-dimensional Weil–Heisenberg space $V$:

1. **Metric resolution (`New.Parameters.sum_pullForm_resolutionLinearForm_apply`):** If $C.\mathrm{area} = |b|^2 M \sqrt{d}/2$, pulling back the one-dimensional hermitian forms $m_j \cdot C.\mathrm{hermitianForm}$ ($(\ell, z) \mapsto \frac{\pi}{C.\mathrm{area}} \bar{\ell} z$) along `resolutionLinearForm (b / P.areaScale) w i` and summing over $I \times n$ recovers the standard hermitian form $u^* v$ identically.
2. **Pullback of monomial sections (`New.Parameters.IsResolutionProduct` & `exists_isResolutionProduct_of_isTightFrame`):** Consider one-dimensional monomial sections $C.\mathrm{monomialSection}(r, t, u) = \theta^r (\sigma^2)^t G^u$ of degree $r + 2t + 3u = m_j$ (where $\theta$ and $\sigma$ are the theta and sigma functions of $C$, and $G = \mathrm{translatedYThetaCube} = y_{\mathrm{tr}} \theta^3$ is entire). For any integer $\delta \in \mathcal{O}_K$ with $\delta \bar{\delta} = N \neq 0$ and any lattice point $a \in C.\Lambda$ with $C.\mathrm{area} = |a|^2 M \sqrt{d} / (2N)$, `exists_isResolutionProduct_of_isTightFrame` constructs a vector $F \in \mathcal{S}(V)$ at scale $b = a / \bar{\delta}$ satisfying:
   - `IsResolutionProduct`: $F \in V.\mathrm{finitePart}$, and for every rational vector $\gamma : n \to E$,
     $$\mathrm{entireTheta}\bigl(\varrho(\mathrm{finVec}(\mathrm{finiteImage}\ \gamma)) F\bigr)(z) = \prod_{i=(j,k)} C.\mathrm{twistedTranslate}\bigl(m_j, \mathrm{resolutionShift}\ b\ w\ \gamma\ i, f_i\bigr)\bigl(\mathrm{resolutionLinearForm}(b/\mathrm{areaScale}, w, i)(z)\bigr).$$
   - Global $p$-adic integrality at finite points (`IsResolutionProduct.norm_iota_apply_le_one`): $\|\iota_p(F(\mathrm{finVec}\ y))\| \le 1$ for every prime $p$ and $y : n \to \mathbb{A}_f(E)$, proved by recognizing finite values as products of torsion values of monomial sections.
   - Periodicity (`IsThetaFunction`): $\mathrm{entireTheta}\ F$ is fixed by signed translations along $\bar{\delta}\mathfrak{a}_0^n$ (built via `normalizedShift_prod_comp_of_mem_lattice`).
3. **Jet factorization (`New.Space.taylor_line_eq_subst_jetSeries` & `jetSeries_eq_prod_of_entireTheta_smul`):** Differentiating under the Gaussian integral (`iteratedDeriv_integral_exp_mul_realVac`) shows that for any $f \in V.\mathrm{finitePart}$, the one-variable Taylor series at $0$ of $t \mapsto \mathrm{entireTheta}\ f (a t v)$ equals `(V.jetSeries a f)` with $v_p X$ substituted for each variable $X_p$ (`taylor_line_eq_subst_jetSeries`). Since a multivariate power series over $\mathbb{C}$ is uniquely determined by its restriction to every line (`mvPowerSeries_eq_of_forall_line`), whenever $\mathrm{entireTheta}\ f (c z) = \prod_i G_i(\mu_i \cdot z)$ for entire functions $G_i$, **`jetSeries_eq_prod_of_entireTheta_smul`** (and `IsResolutionProduct.jetSeries_translate`) gives the exact power-series factorization
   $$V.\mathrm{jetSeries}\ c\ f = \prod_i (\mathrm{taylorAtZero}\ G_i).\mathrm{subst}(\mathrm{linearSeries}\ \mu_i).$$

### 3. Finite-Heisenberg averaging and the integral vacuum $\theta^\circ$

In `src/New/Derivatives/Vacuum/IntegralVacuum.lean` (`lines 60–438`), **`New.Parameters.IsThetaProduct`** specializes `IsResolutionProduct` to the tight frame $(\zeta^k / q_1)_{0 \le k < q_1}$ with multiplicity $q_1$ in every coordinate, recording the value formula `theta_translate`, the jet factorization `jetSeries_translate`, $p$-adic integrality `norm_le_one`, and level periodicity `IsThetaFunction` for $q_1 e \mathfrak{a}_0^n$ (with $V.\theta\ F = 1$ by `IsThetaProduct.theta_eq_one`). Applying `exists_isResolutionProduct_of_isTightFrame` to `P.cmCurve` with period `P.cmPeriod` and $e = 1$ yields **`New.Parameters.exists_isThetaProduct_cmCurve`**, a level-1 theta product $F$ on any standard space $V$.

To descend from the level-$q_1$ theta product $F$ to the level-1 vacuum `V.vacElem`, **`exists_smul_vac_eq_sum`** (specialized to **`IsThetaProduct.exists_smul_vacElem_eq_sum`**) averages over the finite Heisenberg group: there exist a nonzero scalar $c \in \mathbb{C}^\times$ and a finite index set $\Gamma$ of cardinality
$$|\Gamma| = (q_1^2)^{f n}$$
together with finite shifts $a : \Gamma \to n \to \mathbb{A}_f(E)$ and $b : n \to \mathbb{A}_f(E)$ such that
$$c \cdot V.\mathrm{vacElem} = \sum_{j \in \Gamma} V.\mathrm{finHeisenbergOp}(a_j)\bigl(V.\varrho(\mathrm{finVec}\ b) F\bigr).$$

Specializing to rank $n = 1$ (`exists_mul_vacElem_integral_bounded`, `IntegralVacuum.lean:229–280`):
- **Integrality:** Each finite Heisenberg operator multiplies values of $F$ by roots of unity (`norm_map_selfDualAddChar`) and shifts the finite argument, so $p$-adic integrality of $F$ immediately implies $\|\iota_p(c \cdot \mathrm{vacElem}(\mathrm{finVec}\ x))\| \le 1$ at every prime $p$.
- **Archimedean conjugate bound:** By `exists_norm_gal_torsion_value_le`, there is a constant $M$ (depending only on $P$, not on $s$) bounding $\|\sigma(\mathrm{twistedTranslate}\ 1\ (x \Omega_0)\ \theta\ 0)\| \le M$ for all $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/K)$ and $x \in K$. Each finite value of $F$ is a product of $q_1^2$ such torsion values, so $\|\sigma(F(\mathrm{finVec}\ z))\| \le e^{M q_1^2}$. Meanwhile, `sq_q₁_pow_card_infinitePlace_le` bounds the number of summands using $f \le q_1$ and $q_1 \le e^{q_1}$:
  $$(q_1^2)^f \le (e^{q_1})^{2 q_1} = e^{2 q_1^2}.$$
  Summing $|\Gamma|$ terms gives $\|\sigma(c \cdot \mathrm{vacElem}(\mathrm{finVec}\ x))\| \le e^{(\lceil M \rceil + 2) q_1^2}$.

Extracting the constant `vacuumBoundConst` ($C$) and the scalar **`vacuumScale`** ($c_s$) satisfying the structure **`IsVacuumScale`** (`isVacuumScale_vacuumScale`), the rank-$n$ **integral vacuum** is defined as
$$\theta^\circ := \mathrm{intVacuum}\ V = (\mathrm{vacuumScale}\ P\ s)^n \cdot V.\mathrm{vacElem}.$$
It satisfies four core properties used throughout Stage 02 and Stage 06:
- **`norm_intVacuum_le_one`**: $\|\iota_p(\mathrm{intVacuum}\ V\ (\mathrm{finVec}\ x))\| \le 1$ at every prime $p$ and embedding $\iota_p$.
- **`norm_gal_intVacuum_le`** & **`norm_ringEquiv_intVacuum_le`**: $\|\sigma(\mathrm{intVacuum}\ V\ (\mathrm{finVec}\ x))\| \le \exp(C q_1^2)^n$ for every $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/K)$ and indeed every $\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})$ (using `exists_algEquiv_norm_eq`).
- **`exists_intVacuum_ne_zero`**: there exists a finite adelic point $x$ where $\mathrm{intVacuum}\ V\ (\mathrm{finVec}\ x) \neq 0$.
- **`standardData V δ`**: the $\mathcal{O}_{\iota_p}$-submodule of $\mathcal{S}(V)$ spanned by translates of $\theta^\circ$ by points in `integralAfter δ` above $p$—the exact module of integral theta data whose jet divisibility is bounded in [Formal Groups, Theta Normal Form & Prime-Counting Divisibility](./formal-group-and-divisibility.md).

### 4. Galois conjugation of translated jets

Because $c \cdot V.\mathrm{vacElem}$ is a finite Heisenberg sum of translates of the level-1 theta product $F$, **`finitePart_le_span_translates`** (`IntegralVacuum.lean:495–509`) deduces that the translates $\varrho(\mathrm{finVec}\ x) F$ span the entire finite part $V.\mathrm{finitePart}$. Under any $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/K)$, **`map_zetaLinearSeries`** sends the frame linear form `zetaLinearSeries P.cmPeriod (P.jetScale s) k j` to `zetaLinearSeries Ω (σ (P.periodFactor s) / Ω * P.areaScale) k j` up to the permutation `placePerm s σ` of the infinite places. Matching the torsion shifts via `exists_rational_translate_eq` and `IsThetaProduct.isJetConjugate_of_map_taylorAtZero_eq`, and invoking `norm_gal_periodFactor_div_mul_areaScale_sq` for the squared norm of the conjugated scale, produces **`New.Parameters.exists_isJetConjugate_translates`** (`IntegralVacuum.lean:591–654`): there exists a single map $B : \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/K) \to \mathbb{A}_f(E)$ such that on every standard space $V$, there is a spanning vector $F \in V.\mathrm{finitePart}$ ($V.\theta\ F = 1$) whose translates transform under every $\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/K)$ via
$$\mathrm{IsJetConjugate}\ \sigma\ (\mathrm{placePerm}\ s\ \sigma)\ (P.\mathrm{jetScale}\ s)\ t\ \bigl(\varrho(\mathrm{finVec}\ x) F\bigr)\ \bigl(\varrho(\mathrm{finVec}\ (B\ \sigma \cdot x)) F'\bigr)$$
for some $F' \in V.\mathrm{finitePart}$ and scale $t \in \mathbb{C}$ satisfying $|t|^2 = (\mathrm{bigOmega}\ P\ s)^{-1}$.
