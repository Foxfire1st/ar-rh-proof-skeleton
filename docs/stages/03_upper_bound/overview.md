# Stage 03 — Upper Bound: A Composite Theta Lift (Theorem A)

Stage 03 turns the prime-counting derivative divisibility of [Stage 02](../02_derivatives/overview.md) into the upper bound that [Stage 01](../01_reduction/overview.md) consumes as **Theorem A** (`D.span_short_integralRamified_eq_top` and `D.indexLe_normalizedPoincare`). Stage 02 built an integral theta vacuum $\theta^\circ$ and proved that derivatives of theta products over the cyclotomic CM field $E = K(\zeta_{q_1})$ automatically gain $p$-adic divisibility at every non-split prime $p$ with the exact prime-counting weight $\Lambda(n)(1 - \chi_K(n))$. Stage 03 lifts that arithmetic mechanism through an $m$-step chain of unitary theta correspondences so that a single space of small, integral source forms produces target vectors whose archimedean size and prime-by-prime divisibility simultaneously bound the covolume and index of Stage 01's twisted lattice.

## Level 1 — First-Principles

In [Stage 01](../01_reduction/overview.md), the proof compares two measurements of a family of target vectors living inside a {{concept:twisted-lattice|collection of inner-product spaces indexed by twisting characters, each carrying an integral lattice}}: an **upper bound** on how short and divisible the family can be (Theorem A), and a **lower bound** on how large any spanning integral family must be (Theorems B and C). Stage 03 builds the entire upper bound.

The difficulty is geometric. The target vectors in Stage 01 live on an *indefinite* unitary space $Y_0$, where automorphic forms have no finite-sum formula and cannot be differentiated directly using [Stage 02](../02_derivatives/overview.md)'s theta derivatives. Definite unitary spaces $X_0, X_1, \dots, X_m$, by contrast, are built from positive-definite inner products, so their modular forms are finite arrays of algebraic numbers whose values can be controlled both in ordinary Euclidean size and prime by prime.

Stage 03 bridges the indefinite world and the definite world by running two parallel chains of $m$ lifts side by side, each step implemented by a {{concept:theta-correspondence|linear transfer map between modular forms on two unitary groups, defined by integrating against a joint theta function}}:

- **The lower (indefinite-to-definite) chain** starts from a source form $\varphi$ on the definite space $X_0$, converts it into a form $\mathcal{P}\varphi$ on the indefinite space $Y_0$ via the Poincaré map $\mathcal{P}$, and lifts $\mathcal{P}\varphi$ through $m$ steps $Y_0 \to Y_1 \to \cdots \to Y_m$ until the final space $Y_m$ is definite.
- **The upper (all-definite) chain** starts from the same source form $\varphi$ on $X_0$, lifts it through $m$ definite steps $X_0 \to X_1 \to \cdots \to X_m$ using translates of Stage 02's integral vacuum $\theta^\circ$, and restricts the output from $X_m$ to the subspace $Y_m \subset X_m$.

![Two parallel chains of unitary spaces linked at the bottom by the Poincaré map from X_0 to Y_0 and at the top by restriction from the definite space X_m to Y_m](assets/stage03-seesaw-double-chain.svg)

To feed Theorem A into [Stage 01](../01_reduction/overview.md), Stage 03 solves three interlocking problems across five landmark files:

1. **Equating the two chains (`seesaw_identity`):** Using {{concept:seesaw-duality|an integral-pairing symmetry between two dual chains of spaces that exchanges a lift on one side for a restriction on the other}}, Stage 03 unfolds the lower chain one step at a time until both chains agree on $Y_m$ up to a single nonzero scalar $c$ independent of the twist:
   $$\mathrm{lowerChain}_\vartheta(\mathcal{P}\varphi) = c \cdot \mathrm{compositeLift}_\vartheta(\Psi, \varphi).$$
   This transfers every estimate proved on the definite upper chain directly onto the target forms of Stage 01.
2. **Short spanning sources and archimedean size (`short_columns`, `archimedean_size`):** On $X_0$, Stage 03 measures the size of a form's values across all primes using the {{concept:weyl-gauge|prime-by-prime local size norm on modular forms, calling a form integral when this norm is at most $1$ and calling it short of bound $B$ when all its Galois conjugates also have squared norm at most $B$}}. It proves that the {{term:weyl-gauge|B-short}} {{term:weyl-gauge|ramified source forms}} span all {{term:weyl-gauge|ramified source forms}}, and then tracks how each definite lift grows the squared Euclidean norm. At the top step, the restriction $X_m \to Y_m$ injects a saving of $\#\Phi_a \cdot a \log N$ from the level prime $N$ that cancels the excess archimedean growth of the $m$ lifts, bounding the {{concept:gram-determinant|squared volume of a basis of vectors in an inner-product space, controlling the fundamental volume of a lattice}} for Stage 01.
3. **Prime-by-prime divisibility and exceptional inert primes (`elementary_divisor_theorem`, `local_divisibility`):** At every non-split {{concept:prime-number|whole number greater than $1$ with no smaller positive divisors}} $p < x_1$, Stage 02's {{concept:schur-jet|derivative divisibility lower bound on matrix minors and Schur-matrix polynomials}} forces the definite upper chain to be divisible by a large power of $p$. At ordinary non-split primes this gives the full requested divisibility `D.gain p` up to a small error. At certain *exceptional* inert primes, however, shifted {{concept:theta-normal-form|Galois-paired infinite places where cross-place couplings collide}} cause extra deductions. Stage 03 cancels those collisions by pre-composing the source forms with a {{concept:hecke-operator|finite averaging operator on modular forms built from matrix cosets at a prime}} whose action stays nearly onto modulo high powers of $p$. Measuring that index loss requires an abstract theorem on {{concept:elementary-divisor|the prime-power diagonal factors governing the quotient index of a linear operator's image inside an integral lattice}}, proved via a polynomial-counting machine over a {{concept:frobenius-cycle|cyclic family of Frobenius-twisted spaces with controlled polynomial defect}}.

Together, these three pillars prove that after dividing by the exact inverse gain factor $c_B = \prod p^{-g_p}$ and the seesaw constant $c_{\mathrm{ss}}$, the normalized Poincaré images of {{term:weyl-gauge|B-short}} {{term:weyl-gauge|ramified source forms}} span the source space, have bounded archimedean size, and land in the integral lattice of Stage 01 with total index bounded by the allowed loss sum.

### Where this analogy breaks

Describing Stage 03 as "proving an identity between two chains, then estimating size and divisibility on the easier chain" omits two essential logical boundaries. First, the seesaw identity `New.Data.seesaw_identity` proves that the proportionality constant $c \in \mathbb{C}^\times$ is nonzero and independent of the twist, **not** that either side is nonzero; proving that the Poincaré images actually span a large subspace of target forms belongs to Theorem B in [Stage 04 (`04_spanning`)](../04_spanning/overview.md). Second, Stage 03 is not a self-contained leaf behind Stage 01: its `short_columns` module defines the prime-sum gains, exceptional primes, and ramified source forms used across the rest of the proof, including an acyclic backward dependency from Stage 02's sharpness file `02_derivatives/bound_attained`.

### 1. The double chain and the iterated seesaw (`Objects/`, `Seesaw/SeesawIdentity.lean`)

Over the cyclotomic CM field $E = K(\zeta_{q_1})$ with $f = |D.\Phi|$ infinite places, admissible data $D$ fixes $m = 2\varrho + 2$ lifts, base ranks $r$ and $k = r + 1 - m$, step increment $t = 5r$, and a prime level $N = \pi_N \bar{\pi}_N$.
- **Upper definite chain:** $X_0 \to X_1 \to \cdots \to X_m$, where each $X_j$ is a standard definite Hermitian space of rank $n_j = k + j(t + 1)$, so $n_m = k + m(t + 1) = r + mt + 1$.
- **Lower chain:** $Y_0 \to Y_1 \to \cdots \to Y_m$, where $Y_0$ is formed from $X_0$ by adjoining $\varrho$ hyperbolic planes and one line of norm $-N$ (rank $r$), and each $Y_j$ has rank $r + jt$ with signature shifting until $Y_m \subset X_m$ is definite of rank $r + mt = n_m - 1$.

The Poincaré map $\mathcal{P} = D.\mathrm{poincareAt}\ g\ t : \mathcal{A}(X_0) \to \mathcal{A}(Y_0)$ is the Riesz representative of the Bessel period on $Y_0$. Pairing the lower chain $\Theta_\vartheta(\mathcal{P}\varphi)$ with a conjugate form on $Y_m$ and passing each theta lift to its transpose (`transposedLiftY`) unfolds the Bessel period one step at a time into a finite weighted coset sum (`cutSum`). Collecting the translated vacuum data from those cosets yields a jointly invariant tensor `seesawDatum ϑ`, which rescales to an admissible chain datum $\Psi$ satisfying `New.Data.seesaw_identity`:
$$\exists c \in \mathbb{C}^\times,\; \forall \vartheta \in D.\mathrm{Twist},\; \exists \Psi,\; \mathrm{IsChainDatum}\ \vartheta\ \Psi \;\wedge\; \forall \varphi \in D.\mathrm{ramifiedSourceForms},\; \mathrm{lowerChain}\ \vartheta\ (\mathcal{P}\varphi) = c \cdot \mathrm{compositeLift}\ \vartheta\ \Psi\ \varphi.$$

### 2. Short columns, prime-sum bookkeeping, and archimedean size (`Size/ShortColumns.lean`, `Size/ArchimedeanSize.lean`)

- **`ShortColumns.lean`** defines the local prime sum $s_p(y, \sigma) = v(\pi_p)\sum_{e \in I_p} (y/p^e - \kappa(-p^e\sigma))_+$, the total $F(p) = \sum_\sigma s_p(\kappa(\sigma), \sigma)$ (`localPrimeSumTotal`), the requested chain divisibility
  $$\mathrm{gain}(p) = m\Bigl(\sum_{\tau \in \Phi_a} s_p(a, \tau) + k\, F(p)\Bigr),$$
  the allowed loss unit $\mathrm{loss}(p) = f a (\log b)^2 / p$ (multiplied by $m$ when $p \equiv \pm 1 \pmod m$), and the notion of an **exceptional** inert prime (`IsExceptional`: $\chi_K(p) = -1$, $p \not\equiv \pm 1 \pmod m$, and the order $o_0$ of $-p$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/q_0\mathbb{Z})^\times$ is odd with $o_0 \le \lceil \log_p m \rceil$). Using Weyl evaluation vectors and the ramifying operator $\prod_{w \mid \pi_N}(1 - z_w)$ on central scalings, it proves that $B$-short forms span all source forms (`span_isShort_eq_top`, $B = \exp(\mathrm{errorUnit})/4^f$) and all ramified source forms (`span_isShort_ramifiedSourceForms_eq_top`, $B = \exp(\mathrm{errorUnit})$), while ramified forms retain proportion at least $1 - f/(N-1) \ge 1 - b^{-8}$ of the dimension.
- **`ArchimedeanSize.lean`** bounds the squared Weyl norm of every Galois conjugate $\sigma$ of one definite theta kernel (`sqrt_weylNormSq_jetMap_le`) by $\exp(x^{50} + \mathrm{rowSizeSum}_j / 2)$ and sums over $j < m$ to obtain `chainSize` $= m(\#\Phi_a s_\infty(a) + k\sum_\tau s_\infty(\kappa(\tau)))$ up to $\mathrm{errorUnit}/2$. At the top restriction $X_m \to Y_m$, the branching factor $\prod_{\tau \in \Phi_a} \bar{\pi}_N^a$ saves $\#\Phi_a a \log N$, cancelling the archimedean excess $m(s_\infty(a) - s_{\mathrm{main}}) - a\log N \le 32 a \log b$ (`mul_jetSize_sub_mainPrimeSum_sub_le`). Rewriting the prime sums via `two_mul_sum_gain_mul_log` proves **`weylNormSq_compositeLift_le`**: if $\|\sigma\varphi\|^2 \le \exp(B)$, then
  $$\mathrm{weylNormSq}\bigl(\sigma \circ \mathrm{compositeLift}\ 1\ \Psi\ \varphi\bigr) \le \exp\!\Bigl(B + h_{\mathrm{main}} - m\,\mathrm{rem} + 2\sum_{p \in \text{small nonsplit}} \mathrm{gain}(p)\log p + \mathrm{errorUnit}\Bigr).$$
  It then passes both integrality and size through the good projection $|\bar{G}| e_0 s$ (`goodProjTranslate_mem_integralForms`, `normSqAt_goodProjTranslate_le`).

### 3. Prime-by-prime divisibility and the elementary-divisor machine (`ElementaryDivisors/ElementaryDivisorTheorem.lean`, `Divisibility/LocalDivisibility.lean`)

- **`LocalDivisibility.lean`** bounds the $p$-adic gauge of `compositeLift ϑ Ψ φ` for every prime $p$ coprime to $\vartheta.\mathrm{normQ}$ (`exists_colengthDvd_gaugeLe_compositeLift`):
  - For primes outside `smallNonsplitPrimes` with $p \neq N$ ($p$ split or $p \ge x_1$, which automatically forces $p \neq d$), `gaugeLe_restrictedChain_one` gives gauge $\le 1$ on $S = M$ with $l = 0$; at the level prime $p = N$ (where $x_1 \le N$ gives `gain N = 0`), `ColengthAtLevel` and `ValuationAtLevel` pull back `upIntegralForms` with $l \le m f b \dim \le 2 m f b \dim$ and valuation loss at most $2 m f b$.
  - For non-split $p < x_1$, Stage 02's `norm_schurJet_jetSeries_le_of_charges` gives valuation at least $\mathrm{gain}(p) - \mathrm{loss}(p) - m\cdot \mathrm{collisionSum}(p, \emptyset)$ (`gaugeLe_restrictedChain_collisionSum`). When $p$ is not exceptional, the collision sum is already negligible.
  - When $p < x_1$ is **exceptional**, collisions occur between $a$-places $\tau$ and $-p^e\tau$. Picking a **full mark** $\Lambda$ of primes above $p$ (disjoint from $\bar{\Lambda}$ and meeting every conjugate pair induced by $a$-places) and forming the product $Y = \prod_{\mathfrak{l} \in \Lambda} T_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\mathrm{scaled}}$ of scaled Hecke operators restores full valuation $\ge \mathrm{gain}(p) - 2\,\mathrm{loss}(p)$ on $Y^m M + p^{j_p} M$ (`gaugeLe_compositeLift_of_isFullMark`).
- **`ElementaryDivisorTheorem.lean`** supplies the key colength control proving that $Y^m M + p^{j_p} M$ has small index in the integral source module $M$ (`isOntoUpToColength_prod_scaledHeckeOperator_pow`, colength dividing $p^l$ with $l \le \mathrm{loss}(p)\cdot \dim \mathcal{A}(X_0)$). Working over a valuation domain $O$ and a `FrobeniusCycle` $T$, it factors the edge Hecke operator $A$ through functions on edges, constructs a near inverse `nearInverse` via Levi averaging (`AveragingMap`), bounds the defect space using Stepanov's root count (`RootLemma`, `DefectCount`), and proves **`New.FrobeniusCycle.elementary_divisors`**: the colength of $Y L + p^m L$ in $L$ divides $p^l$ with $l \le (3 c_{\mathrm{div}} + \theta m)|X||I|$.
